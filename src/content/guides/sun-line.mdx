## What is the sun line in palmistry?

The sun line is a line that runs up the palm towards the ring finger. In Hindi it is <Hi>सूर्य रेखा</Hi> (surya rekha). Cheiro calls it the line of Sun, the line of Apollo, the line of brilliancy or the line of success, and Dale’s *Indian Palmistry* (1895) calls it the Via Solis. They are all the same line.

It ends on the mount of the Sun, the pad under the ring finger, and runs up beside the [fate line](/fate-line/), on its little-finger side. Benham (1900) describes it rising, when long, from the upper part of the mount of the Moon on the outer side of the palm; when short, it starts higher up, and sometimes it doesn’t reach the finger at all.

The PalmSays scanner traces the four major lines, not the sun line, so the photo at the top shows none. Every sun line on this page is a **drawing**, in gold, of a form the books describe.

<KeyPoint>A sun line can be long, short, doubled or missing. Each form has a reading, and none of them is a forecast.</KeyPoint>

## Sun line types and their meanings

Match your own hand to the cards. Each meaning is in our own words, from the books named under it. The old books promise luck, riches and honours from many of these forms; like the PalmSays app, we keep only the part that describes a tendency.

### Where does your sun line start?

Cheiro lists where the sun line can rise: from the life line, the fate line, the middle of the palm (the plain of Mars), the mount of the Moon, the head line or the heart line, or only on its own mount under the ring finger.

### How clear is your sun line?

Cheiro reads a bright, warm manner that draws people in and gives you some sway with them. Dale reads a straight, unbroken sun line as a manner that tends to win the goodwill of senior and influential people. Both books also promise honours; that promise is not repeated.

Dale reads an uneven, broken sun line as obstacles and envy from others. The app keeps it as a pattern the tradition describes: friction when you put your work forward, not a forecast about anyone. A crease or a shadow can also make a line look broken.

### What does no sun line mean?

<Variations>
<Variation variant="sun-absent" title="No sun line" gist="Normal. Markun, quoting other palmists, says a missing sun line does not mean poverty: at most it shows someone careless with small sums. Cheiro reads a missing sun line darkly; we don’t repeat that. Benham (1900) warns that this line has been badly misread, and that some careful students stopped using it.

</Variation>
</Variations>

## Is the sun line the success line?

Yes: “line of success” is one of its old names. But Benham (1900) calls it one of the most misunderstood lines on the hand. Palmists who saw a good sun line used to tell people they had great artistic gifts or would win fame and wealth, and clients were let down. He concludes that it had better be dropped than read that extravagantly.

Cheiro himself notes that the chief thing the line shows, when well marked, is a sensitive nature. So the honest reading is about temperament: how much beauty and surroundings matter to you, and how you come across to people. The PalmSays app reads it the same way. The sun line is also one of the lines people call the [money line](/money-line/), and [career palmistry](/career-palmistry/) reads it beside the fate line.

## Sun line vs fate line

The [fate line](/fate-line/) runs up the middle of the palm towards the middle finger and is read for direction and work. The sun line runs beside it, towards the ring finger, and is read for how beauty and surroundings affect you and how you come across. A sun line often starts from the fate line or runs parallel to it.

Cheiro says both lines must be read with the shape of the hand: a sun line is more heavily marked on philosophic, conic and psychic hands, and means less there than the same line on a square or spatulate hand. The [hand types in palmistry](/hand-types/) guide explains those shapes.

## Marks on the sun line

The old books read small marks on this line too. Cheiro reads a star as very good fortune, a square as protection for your name and position, and an island or a cross as trouble for them. Those are promises and threats about events, so we leave them out. The mystic cross and other [crosses on the palm](/palm-crosses/) have their own guide, and the [palmistry terms](/palmistry-terms/#sun-line) glossary explains each mark in a sentence.

These marks are also usually only a few pixels wide in a phone photo, too small to see reliably. What a good photo does show is whether the line is there, how long it is, and whether it breaks or forks.

## Sun line in the left and right hand, for women and men

The sun line is read the same way for everyone; there is no separate sun line for women or men. What changes is which hand you look at. Tradition reads your writing hand as the life you are making and the other as what you started with, so for a right-handed person the sun line on the right hand is the one read for the life you are making. Compare both. [Which hand to read](/which-hand-to-read/) explains the traditions.

## Myths about the sun line

<MythReality>
<Myth claim="No sun line means no success.">

A missing sun line is normal. Even Markun’s old book says it does not mean poverty, and no line decides success.

</Myth>
<Myth claim="The sun line shows how rich you will be.">

The books that promised riches from it are the reason Benham called it misunderstood. The app reads it for temperament only.

</Myth>
<Myth claim="A sun line means you are an artist.">

At most it is read as a feel for beauty and surroundings. Cheiro says that may show only as a love of beautiful things around you.

</Myth>
</MythReality>
