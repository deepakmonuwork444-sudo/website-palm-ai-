## Fate line types and their meanings

<DiagramFigure id="fate-line-start-end-and-types" />

The books read the fate line mostly by **where it starts** and **where it ends**. Match your own line to the cards.

### Where does your fate line start and end?

<CompareRow framed forms={[['fate-wrist', 'From the wrist'], ['fate-from-life', 'From the life line'], ['fate-from-luna', 'From the outer palm'], ['fate-late', 'From mid-palm'], ['fate-from-head', 'From the head line']]} />

<Variations>
<Variation variant="fate-wrist" title="Starting at the wrist" gist="Responsibility that arrives early: a sense of duty towards work." check="Does a line rise from your wrist straight up the middle of your palm?" alt="A fate line rising from the wrist straight up the middle of the palm" src={['markun-what-you-should-know-about-palmistry-1927', 'cheiro-guide-to-the-hand-1900']}>

Responsibility that arrives early: a sense of duty towards work that starts young. When the line runs straight up to the middle finger, Cheiro’s *Guide to the Hand* reads a strong, self-directed personality that pushes past obstacles on its own path. Some old books also promise great success for this form; that promise is left out.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="fate-from-life" title="Starting from the life line" gist="Direction won by your own effort, after early years shaped by family." check="Does the line start on your life line and rise from there?" alt="A fate line that begins on the life line and rises up the palm" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916']}>

Cheiro reads direction won by your own effort and merit, after early years shaped a great deal by family and surroundings. He adds that the point where the line leaves the life line marks a year of independence; we never read dates from a palm.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="fate-from-luna" title="Starting from the outer palm (mount of the Moon)" gist="A path shaped a great deal by other people: the public, patrons or partners." check="Does the line rise from the lower outer edge of your palm, opposite the thumb?" alt="A fate line rising from the lower outer edge of the palm and curving towards the middle finger" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916', 'cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900', 'markun-what-you-should-know-about-palmistry-1927', 'frith-practical-palmistry-1895']}>

A path shaped a great deal by other people: work where the public, patrons or partners play a large part. It describes where influence comes from, not whether it helps.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="fate-late" title="Starting in the middle of the palm" gist="The self-made pattern: what you build comes from your own persistence." check="Does the line only begin around the middle of your palm?" alt="A fate line that only begins in the middle of the palm" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916', 'cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900']}>

The self-made pattern: what you build tends to come from your own persistence rather than an easy start. The books promise eventual success here; that promise is not repeated, because it describes effort, not an outcome.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="fate-from-venus" title="Starting inside the life line (mount of Venus)" gist="A working life shaped a great deal by love and close family." check="Does the line rise from the base of your thumb, inside your life line?" alt="A fate line rising from the base of the thumb, crossing the life line and running up the palm" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916', 'cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900']}>

A line that rises from the pad at the base of the thumb, inside the life line. Cheiro reads a working life strongly shaped by love and close relationships, and *Cheiro’s Language of the Hand* reads a line tied close beside the life line, low down, as early years given to the wishes of parents or relatives. Both describe where influence comes from, not how things turn out. Cheiro’s further claims about marriage are left out.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="fate-from-head" title="Starting at the head line" gist="Direction found later, through your own talents and hard work." check="Does the line only begin where it meets your head line, in the middle of your palm?" alt="A fate line that begins at the head line and runs up towards the middle finger" src={['cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900']}>

*Cheiro’s Language of the Hand* reads direction that is found later in life, after real effort and through your own talents. The book promises success at the end of that road; we keep the effort, not the promise.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="fate-from-heart" title="Starting at the heart line" gist="Direction that settles very late, after long effort; Cheiro also reads a taste for art." check="Does the line only begin at your heart line, just below the middle finger?" alt="A short fate line that begins at the heart line and runs up towards the middle finger" src={['cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900']}>

A short line that only begins at the heart line. *Cheiro’s Language of the Hand* reads a direction that settles very late, after a long, difficult effort, and adds a strong taste for art and artistic things. As with every start point, it describes a pattern of effort, not a date or a result.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="fate-end-index" title="Ending under the index finger" gist="Effort pulled towards responsibility and leading others." check="Does the top of the line bend towards your index finger?" alt="A fate line that bends towards the index finger" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916', 'cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900']}>

Effort pulled towards responsibility and leading others: ambition that looks for a position of trust. The old books call this a sign of success; here it describes where effort tends to go, not what it will bring.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="fate-end-ring" title="Ending under the ring finger" gist="Effort drawn towards visible, public-facing work." check="Does the top of the line bend towards your ring finger?" alt="A fate line that bends towards the ring finger" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916']}>

Effort drawn towards visible, public-facing work, where being recognised matters. Cheiro reads a line bending towards the little finger instead as effort drawn to trade, business or skilled technical work.

</Variation>
</Variations>

A line that ends under the **middle finger**, its usual place, is read by Dale’s *Indian Palmistry* as a reflective, deliberate approach to work, with decisions thought through before they are acted on. An unbroken line ending there she reads as a quiet, serious-minded nature, the kind of person others go to for considered advice. The fate line can also start **inside** the life line, on the thumb mound: Cheiro reads close relationships and strong feelings weighing heavily on the choices you make about work.

### How clear is your fate line?

<CompareRow framed forms={[['fate-wrist', 'Deep and clear'], ['fate-faint', 'Faint'], ['fate-absent', 'None']]} />

<Variations>
<Variation variant="fate-faint" title="Faint fate line" gist="A strong preference for choosing your own path." check="Is the line thin and faint, hard to see even in daylight?" alt="A thin, faint fate line up the middle of the palm" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916']}>

A strong preference for self-direction: little patience with the idea that anything but your own choices decides your path. A **deep, heavily marked** line is read the other way, as a steady, routine-shaped working life. Markun warns against reading a heavily marked fate line as luck, and we don’t.

</Variation>
</Variations>

### Does your fate line break, fork or branch?

<CompareRow framed forms={[['fate-broken', 'Broken'], ['fate-double', 'Double'], ['fate-fork', 'Forked at the start'], ['fate-wavy', 'Wavy']]} />

<Variations>
<Variation variant="fate-broken" title="Broken fate line" gist="A working life that changes direction, not one straight road." check="Does the line stop and start again, with the pieces overlapping?" alt="A fate line with a gap where the two ends overlap" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916', 'markun-what-you-should-know-about-palmistry-1927', 'raphael-cheirosophy-1901', 'jain-samudrik-shastra-1927']}>

A working life that changes direction: a real change of job or surroundings rather than one straight road. The books disagree on whether such a change turns out well, and neither view is assumed here. Jain reads a line seen in pieces as quick and capable but restless, where staying with one course is harder than starting one.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="fate-double" title="Double fate line" gist="Two lines side by side: the fate line’s qualities made stronger." check="Do two lines run side by side up the middle of your palm?" alt="Two fate lines running side by side up the palm" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916']}>

Two lines running side by side. The app has no separate rule for this. Cheiro’s general rule for a sister line, one running close beside another, is that it strengthens the line it runs with, so the tradition reads a double fate line as the fate line’s qualities made stronger.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="fate-fork" title="Forked fate line" gist="Two starts, from the thumb side and the outer palm: pulled between feeling and imagination." check="Does the line start in two branches, one from each side of your lower palm?" alt="A fate line that starts in two branches, one from the base of the thumb and one from the outer palm, joining in the middle" src={['cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900', 'frith-practical-palmistry-1895']}>

A fate line that starts in two branches, one rising from the base of the thumb and one from the lower outer palm. *Cheiro’s Language of the Hand* reads a path that swings between imagination on one side and love and feeling on the other. Frith reads a fork at the base as an unsettled, undecided start in life. The books give no clear reading for a fork at the top of the line.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="fate-wavy" title="Wavy fate line" gist="A course that keeps changing rather than running straight." check="Does the line wander from side to side as it runs up your palm?" alt="A fate line that wanders from side to side as it runs up the palm" src={['benham-laws-of-scientific-hand-reading-1900', 'cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916']}>

Benham reads a wavy fate line (his line of Saturn) as a course that keeps changing, and Cheiro’s rule for any wavy line is uncertainty and a lack of decision. Many working lives change course often, by choice as much as by chance; the line says nothing about whether that goes well.

</Variation>
</Variations>

A **fork** or a **wavy** stretch in the fate line is harder to read from a photo, because the fate line crosses the head and heart lines and branches often turn out to be other creases. The PalmSays app reads breaks in the fate line, but it has no separate reading for a forked or wavy one.

## Marks on the fate line

Like the other lines, the fate line can carry tiny marks: squares, islands and crosses. Most are only a few pixels wide in a phone photo, so the PalmSays app leaves them out. The books read them like this:

- **Square:** *Cheiro’s Language of the Hand* reads a square on the fate line as protection from loss in work and money matters, as a square is protective on every line. It is a symbol in the books, not a promise.
- **Island or cross:** the old books read these as hard stretches in working life. Those readings forecast events, so we leave them out.

## No fate line? It’s common

Many palms show the fate line faintly, in short pieces, or not at all. That is very common, and it isn’t read as a lack of luck or purpose.

<KeyPoint>No fate line is not bad luck. It is read as a path that isn’t fixed in advance.</KeyPoint>

The old books themselves disagree about a missing fate line. One judges it harshly; another notes that people without it often do well. The PalmSays rule set shows neither judgement as a prediction. What it keeps is the gentler reading: your direction isn’t set out in advance, and work tends to follow your circumstances and choices as they come, rather than one fixed track.

A fate line can also be missing from a photo when it is simply faint, so a careful reading says “not clearly seen” rather than “none”.

## How to tell the fate line from the life line

The fate line is the vertical line up the middle of your palm; the life line curves around the thumb. To find it:

<PhotoSteps line="fate" />

1. Start near the wrist, in the middle of your palm.
2. Look for a line running up through the centre, past the head and heart lines.
3. Follow it towards the base of your middle finger. In Hindi it is <Hi>भाग्य रेखा</Hi> (bhagya rekha).

It doesn’t always start at the wrist. It may rise out of the [life line](/life-line/), from the lower outer edge of the palm, or only from the middle of the palm. It may also fade out partway up, or stop at the head or heart line. All of these are common.

## Is it the career line or the luck line?

It is the same line. People call the fate line the **destiny line**, the **career line**, the **job line** or the **luck line**. Dale’s *Indian Palmistry* (1895) calls it the Line of Saturn, after the mount under the middle finger where it usually ends. In Jain’s Hindi book it is the upward line, <Hi>ऊर्ध्वरेखा</Hi> (urdhva rekha).

Don’t mix it up with the sun line, a line some palms show beside it, rising towards the ring finger instead of the middle finger: see [sun line meaning](/sun-line/). Whatever the name, the books read the fate line for **how you approach work and direction**. None of them can tell you which job you will get, how much you will earn or how lucky you will be.

## The fate line and your work

Because people call it the career line, the fate line gets asked the biggest questions: which job, how much money, when. The books read it for **how** you approach work: early responsibility, your own effort, other people’s influence, a change of direction. That can be a useful prompt to think about what you want from your working life. It can’t make the decision for you. [Career palmistry](/career-palmistry/) reads the fate line together with the head line, the sun line and the shape of the hand, and the [money line](/money-line/) guide explains why no line shows income.

### Does the fate line show when you’ll succeed?

No. Cheiro’s *Palmistry for All* (1916) divides the fate line, like the life line, into seven-year periods and reads dates from them. We don’t: a crease can’t carry a date, and no line can say whether or when success will come. What the books describe is how you approach work, and that part is yours to use.

## Fate line in a woman’s and a man’s hand

The fate line is read the same way for everyone. The “job line in a female hand” or “lucky line in a female hand” that people search for is the same fate line, with the same readings. What traditions differ on is only which hand to read. [Which hand to read](/which-hand-to-read/) explains the writing-hand tradition and the Indian custom of reading a woman’s left hand.

## Myths about the fate line

<MythReality>
<Myth claim="No fate line means no luck or no career.">

Many palms show a faint fate line or none. That is common, and the tradition doesn’t read it as a bad sign: the gentler reading is simply a path that isn’t fixed in advance.

</Myth>
<Myth claim="A deep fate line means you will be lucky.">

Markun warns against reading a heavily marked fate line as luck. It is read as a steady, routine-shaped working life.

</Myth>
<Myth claim="A broken fate line means failure.">

A break is read as a change of direction, and the books themselves disagree about whether it turns out well. Changing work is common and often chosen.

</Myth>
</MythReality>
