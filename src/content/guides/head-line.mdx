## Where is the head line?

The head line runs across the middle of your palm, below the [heart line](/heart-line/). Start at the edge of your palm between the thumb and the index finger. The head line begins there, often joined to the [life line](/life-line/) for a short way before the two separate. It then runs across the palm, either fairly straight or sloping down towards the lower outer edge.

People also call it the **mind line** or **brain line**. In Hindi it is <Hi>मस्तिष्क रेखा</Hi> (mastishk rekha).

**Head line or heart line?** The heart line starts from the little-finger edge and sits higher, just under the fingers. The head line starts from the thumb side and sits lower. If you only see one straight line across the top half of your palm, read about [when the heart and head lines join](#when-the-heart-and-head-lines-join).

## Head line types and their meanings

Readers look at four things: **how straight or sloping** the line is, **how long** it is, **where it starts** (joined to the life line or not) and **where it ends**. Your head line may fit several of the types below at once.

<Variations>
<Variation variant="head-straight" title="Straight head line" alt="A head line running straight across the palm" src={['cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900', 'cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916', 'benham-laws-of-scientific-hand-reading-1900', 'cheiro-guide-to-the-hand-1900']}>

Practical common sense: a mind that prefers the concrete and can be relied on to carry a decision through. Several books add settled opinions: once a view is formed, it tends to stay. One reads a line with no curve at all as unyielding, which we take as a caution, not a verdict.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="head-gentle" title="Gently sloping head line" alt="A head line that slopes gently downwards across the palm" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916', 'cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900', 'cheiro-guide-to-the-hand-1900', 'saint-germain-practice-of-palmistry-1900']}>

Imagination kept in hand: creative thinking used when it is wanted, on a practical footing. The books call it level-headed, with logic and imagination in balance so neither runs the show alone.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="head-sloping" title="Deeply sloping (curved) head line" alt="A head line that curves steeply down towards the lower outer edge of the palm" src={['cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900', 'cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916', 'desbarrolles-chiromancie-nouvelle-1859', 'markun-what-you-should-know-about-palmistry-1927']}>

A strongly imaginative, idealistic mind that does its best work when inspiration carries it. The books point it towards artistic, literary or intellectual work, and read a line ending low on the outer palm as a pull towards the unusual. Old books attach darker claims to an extreme slope; those are refused here.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="head-long" title="Long head line" alt="A long head line reaching almost to the outer edge of the palm" src={['cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900', 'markun-what-you-should-know-about-palmistry-1927', 'st-hill-grammar-of-palmistry-1893', 'frith-practical-palmistry-1895']}>

Wide intellectual reach: able to take on large, complex subjects and follow them a long way. When the long line is also unbroken, the books read sound judgement and a steady mind; when it is long and straight, a logical mind that is careful with resources.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="head-short" title="Short head line" alt="A short head line that stops near the middle of the palm" src={['cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900', 'markun-what-you-should-know-about-palmistry-1927']}>

A practical, hands-on mind, most at home with concrete tasks and able to focus closely on one specialty. It is not read as low intelligence. One old book reads a very short line far more darkly; that reading is refused here.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="head-fork" title="Forked head line (the writer’s fork)" alt="A head line that splits into two branches near its end, one dipping downwards" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916', 'cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900', 'markun-what-you-should-know-about-palmistry-1927', 'benham-laws-of-scientific-hand-reading-1900', 'st-hill-grammar-of-palmistry-1893']}>

Two ways of thinking at once, the practical and the imaginative. That gives range and tact, and can make choosing harder: the old advice is to trust your first impulse. When one branch dips towards the lower outer palm, Cheiro reads a talent for imaginative writing and storytelling, hence the name.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="head-joined" title="Head line joined to the life line" alt="A head line that starts joined to the life line before the two separate" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916', 'cheiro-guide-to-the-hand-1900', 'benham-laws-of-scientific-hand-reading-1900', 'frith-practical-palmistry-1895', 'dale-indian-palmistry-1895']}>

The more common form, and one where the traditions differ. Cheiro and several others read a sensitive, careful mind that weighs other people’s advice before its own, with self-reliance that grows later; Frith adds a shyness often hidden behind a quick manner. Dale’s *Indian Palmistry* reads good wit, an even temper and a sharp practical mind.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="head-separate" title="Head line not joined to the life line" alt="A head line that starts a little above the life line, with a gap between them" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916', 'cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900', 'st-hill-grammar-of-palmistry-1893', 'frith-practical-palmistry-1895']}>

Independent thinking and quick judgement, with a streak of mental daring: at its best with a clear purpose to aim at. The books add that this goes with impulsiveness, so second thoughts are often the better ones. A very wide gap is read as boldness that can run ahead of careful thought.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="head-broken" title="Broken head line" alt="A head line with a gap where the two ends overlap" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916']}>

A break is not read as a warning. Cheiro’s rule for any broken line is that the break interrupts what the line shows at that point, and that where the two ends overlap its qualities carry on. So no separate trait is read from the break. Older books attach darker claims; they are refused here.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="head-chained" title="Chained or faint head line" alt="A head line drawn as a row of small links" src={['cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900', 'cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916']}>

A chained line is read as ideas that shift before they settle, so decisions take longer. A faint line, lying on the surface rather than cut deep, as a mind that ranges more than it concentrates. Neither is a measure of intelligence, and claims about the mind’s health are refused.

</Variation>
</Variations>

A **deep, clearly marked** head line is read as concentration and a retentive memory: thinking that goes deep rather than wide. Dale reads a clear, unbroken line as a cheerful, inventive turn of mind.

## Where the head line starts and ends

Cheiro’s books, and Benham for the outer edge, also read where the head line starts and ends. The PalmSays app uses these readings:

- **Starting under the index finger:** ambition joined to judgement, a mind drawn to organising and taking charge.
- **Starting inside the life line**, on the thumb side: a sensitive mind that feels friction with other people keenly.
- **Ending under the middle finger:** depth of thought, with an interest in music or questions of faith.
- **Ending under the ring finger:** a wish to be noticed and known for your ideas.
- **Curving up towards the little finger:** a mind drawn to commerce and science. This is an attitude to money, never a forecast of it.
- **Ending on the outer edge of the palm, fairly level:** practical ideas and the balanced middle course. Cheiro adds strong will and quiet determination when the line starts joined to the life line.

## Two head lines?

Some palms show a second line running alongside the head line. That is a double head line, which is uncommon and different from a fork. We explain how to tell them apart in [two head lines on your palm](/head-line/double/).

## Education line (vidya rekha)

Some readers today talk of an “education line”, <Hi>विद्या रेखा</Hi> (vidya rekha). The classical books we use don’t name such a line. The English books read thinking style from the head line itself, and Indian texts such as Jain’s *Samudrik Shastra* (1927) link learning to a small barleycorn-shaped mark instead. That mark is far too small for a phone photo. Either way, no line can predict exams, a degree or success.

## Marks on the head line

A good photo shows the head line’s **breaks**, **forks** and **branches**, and the types above cover those. Islands, chains of tiny loops, dots, crosses, stars and squares are usually only a few pixels wide in a phone photo. An honest reading leaves them out rather than guessing at them.

## Head line in the left and right hand, for women and men

The head line is read the same way for everyone; there is no separate head line for women or men. Compare both hands. Tradition reads the hand you write with as the life you are making and the other as what you started with, so a difference between the two is read as a change in how you think. [Which hand to read](/which-hand-to-read/) explains the traditions in full.

## When the heart and head lines join

Sometimes the heart and head lines form one straight crease across the palm instead of two lines. Palmistry calls it the simian line; doctors call it a single palmar crease, which is usually a normal variation. Benham classes a single line sitting where the head line should be as a head line. The [simian line guide](/simian-line/) gives the medical facts first, then what the books say.

## Myths about the head line

<MythReality>
<Myth claim="A long head line means you are clever, and a short one means you aren’t.">

Length is read as the range of a thinking style, not its power. A short line is read as a focused, practical mind.

</Myth>
<Myth claim="A broken head line means a breakdown or an accident.">

The books that make claims like this are refused. Cheiro’s rule for any broken line is that the qualities carry on where the ends overlap, and a crease or shadow can make a line look broken in a photo.

</Myth>
<Myth claim="The education line shows how far you’ll study.">

The classical books don’t name an education line, and no line can predict exams or degrees.

</Myth>
</MythReality>
