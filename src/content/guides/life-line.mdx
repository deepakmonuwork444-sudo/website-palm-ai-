## Does a short life line mean a short life?

No. In palmistry, a short life line is not read as a short life. It is one of the most common fears about palm reading, partly because of the name “life line”. Careful readers don’t read it that way. Markun, and Cheiro’s *Language of the Hand*, warn against reading its length as a count of years, and the PalmSays rule set never does.

What the books do read in the life line is **energy**: how it is spent, how steady it is, and how you meet big changes. A line can also look short when the photo crops it, the light is flat or your thumb is bent.

## Where is your life line?

The life line starts at the edge of your palm between the thumb and the index finger. It often shares its start with the [head line](/head-line/). From there it curves around the fleshy base of the thumb, which the tradition calls the mount of Venus (<Hi>शुक्र पर्वत</Hi>, Shukra parvat), and runs down towards the wrist. In Hindi it is <Hi>जीवन रेखा</Hi> (jeevan rekha).

Don’t confuse it with the [fate line](/fate-line/), which runs straight up the middle of the palm, or with a second, inner line some hands have right beside it (the double life line, below).

## Life line types and their meanings

The books read the life line mostly by its **arc** (how wide it sweeps into the palm), its **clarity** and any **breaks or branches**. None of these is read as years or health.

Energy held closer in: a quieter, less forceful presence, and a reserved, self-contained manner that is slower to reach out for closeness. The books’ claims about health, fertility and length of life are refused.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="life-long" title="Long life line" alt="A long life line that reaches the wrist and curves around the thumb" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916', 'cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900']}>

The form the classical books call normal, read as steady vitality. The same books read its length as a count of years and as health; neither is claimed here.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="life-short" title="Short life line" alt="A life line that stops partway around the thumb" src={['markun-what-you-should-know-about-palmistry-1927', 'cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900']}>

A shorter line. Even the old books warn against reading its length as a count of years, and it is not read that way here. Many people have a short or faint life line on one hand or both.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="life-broken" title="Broken life line" alt="A life line with a gap where one part starts slightly inside the other" src={['markun-what-you-should-know-about-palmistry-1927', 'cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916']}>

Markun reads a break as a period of major change in the way life is lived. Other old books read it far more darkly, and that reading is refused. Cheiro’s rule for broken lines is that where the two ends overlap, the line’s qualities carry on.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="life-double" title="Double life line (sister line)" alt="A life line with a second, shorter line running just inside it, nearer the thumb" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916', 'markun-what-you-should-know-about-palmistry-1927']}>

A second line running just inside the life line. Cheiro calls it the Line of Mars or inner life line, says it is found only on some hands, and reads it as backing up the life line, with a robust, fighting spirit. His claims about illness and danger are left out. Markun treats its old readings lightly.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="life-fork" title="Forked life line" alt="A life line that splits near its end, one branch running towards the outer palm" src={['cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900', 'markun-what-you-should-know-about-palmistry-1927', 'cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916']}>

A branch that runs off towards the lower outer palm (the mount of the Moon) is read as restlessness and a strong wish to travel and see new places. A line that ends at the wrist without a fork is read the other way: a settled life that keeps to familiar ground.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="life-chained" title="Chained life line" alt="A life line drawn as a row of small links" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916']}>

Energy and purpose that come and go rather than holding steady. The books read a chained life line mainly as a sign about the body; that is refused here, and only their general reading of chained lines is kept.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="life-faint" title="Faint or deep life line" alt="A thin, faint life line" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916', 'markun-what-you-should-know-about-palmistry-1927']}>

A faint line is read as a calmer, lower-key energy, steady rather than forceful. A deep one as stamina that comes more from will and nerve than from sheer physical strength. Neither is a health reading, and a photo shows only how faint a line looks.

</Variation>
<Variation variant="life-start-index" title="Life line starting high, under the index finger" alt="A life line that starts high up, close to the base of the index finger" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916', 'cheiro-language-of-the-hand-1900', 'markun-what-you-should-know-about-palmistry-1927', 'raphael-cheirosophy-1901']}>

A life steered by ambition from early on, with good self-command. A line that starts lower, inside the thumb side, is read as a quick temper that takes conscious effort to manage.

</Variation>

## Life line age calculation: why we don’t do it

Some old palmistry books, Cheiro’s among them, read the life line’s length as a count of years. People search for this as the “age line” or “life line age calculation”. Other writers of the same time, Markun among them, warned against reading the line that way. No line can give a date.

So PalmSays never calculates an age, a year or a lifespan from your palm, on this website or in the app. If you have read an age reading somewhere else, you can let it go: it isn’t something a palm can show.

## Life line in the hand for women and men

The life line is read the same way for everyone. There is no separate life line for women or men, and nothing in it is read as a woman’s or a man’s destiny. What traditions differ on is **which hand** to read: the hand you write with is read as the life you are making, and some Indian traditions read a woman’s left hand. [Which hand to read](/which-hand-to-read/) explains this.

## Left and right hand

Compare both hands. The life line often looks different on each: longer, fainter or broken on one side only. Tradition reads your writing hand as what you have built and the other hand as what you started with, so a difference is read as change, not as a problem. It is also common for photos of the two hands to be lit differently.

## Life line vs fate line

The two are easy to mix up where they meet. The life line **curves** around the thumb. The [fate line](/fate-line/) runs **straight up** the middle of the palm towards the middle finger. A fate line sometimes starts from the life line or inside it; the fate line guide explains how the books read that.
