## Does a short life line mean a short life?

No. In palmistry, a short life line is not read as a short life. It is one of the most common fears about palm reading, partly because of the name “life line”. Careful readers don’t read it that way. Markun, and Cheiro’s *Language of the Hand*, warn against reading its length as a count of years, and the PalmSays rule set never does.

<KeyPoint>A short life line is not a short life. The books read this line for energy and how you meet change.</KeyPoint>

What the books do read in the life line is **energy**: how it is spent, how steady it is, and how you meet big changes. A line can also look short when the photo crops it, the light is flat or your thumb is bent.

## Life line types and their meanings

The books read the life line mostly by its **arc** (how wide it sweeps into the palm), its **clarity** and any **breaks or branches**. None of these is read as years or health. Match your own line to the cards.

The curve of the life line marks out the mount of Venus, the fleshy pad at the base of the thumb. Cheiro reads how much room the curve gives it: a line that sweeps well out into the palm is read as more vigour and warmth, a line that hugs the thumb as energy held closer in. Where the line ends is read too: a branch running out towards the lower outer palm is read as restlessness and a love of travel, as in the [forked life line](#type-life-fork) card.

## Marks on the life line

The books describe small marks on the life line: islands, squares, crosses, and fine lines cutting across it. Most are only a few pixels wide in a phone photo, so the PalmSays app leaves them out, and an honest reading does too. Here is what the books say about each, and what we don’t repeat.

- **Island**, a small loop in the line: Cheiro reads it as a sign about health. Palm lines are not a health test, so that reading is left out.
- **Square**, four tiny lines boxing in part of the line, often over a break: Cheiro calls a square “the Mark of Preservation”, a protective sign wherever it appears. In the books it is a symbol, not a promise.
- **Cross**, lying close to the line: *Cheiro’s Language of the Hand* links it with friction among close relatives. Take that as a prompt to think about family ties, not a forecast.

### Lines crossing the life line

Fine lines often run out from the base of the thumb and cut across the life line. Cheiro reads them as worry lines, also called influence lines: other people, often family, pressing on your plans and home life. They are very common, and most hands have a few. The same passage makes darker claims about some of them; those are refused here, because a crease is not an event.

## Life line vs fate line

The life line curves around the base of your thumb. The fate line runs up the middle of the palm. To find yours and tell the two apart:

<PhotoSteps line="life" />

1. Look at the edge of your palm between the thumb and the index finger. The life line starts there, often sharing its start with the [head line](/head-line/).
2. Follow it as it curves around the fleshy base of the thumb, which the tradition calls the mount of Venus (<Hi>शुक्र पर्वत</Hi>, Shukra parvat).
3. It runs down towards the wrist. In Hindi it is <Hi>जीवन रेखा</Hi> (jeevan rekha).

Don’t confuse it with the [fate line](/fate-line/), which runs straight up the middle of the palm, or with a second, inner line some hands have right beside it (the double life line, above).

<FlowChoice pair="life-fate" />

The two are easy to mix up where they meet: the life line **curves** around the thumb, the fate line runs **straight up** towards the middle finger. A fate line sometimes starts from the life line or inside it; the fate line guide explains how the books read that.

## Life line age calculation: why we don’t do it

Some old palmistry books, Cheiro’s among them, read the life line’s length as a count of years. People search for this as the “age line” or “life line age calculation”. Other writers of the same time, Markun among them, warned against reading the line that way. No line can give a date.

So PalmSays never calculates an age, a year or a lifespan from your palm, on this website or in the app. If you have read an age reading somewhere else, you can let it go: it isn’t something a palm can show.

## Life line in the hand for women and men

The life line is read the same way for everyone. There is no separate life line for women or men, and nothing in it is read as a woman’s or a man’s destiny. What traditions differ on is **which hand** to read: the hand you write with is read as the life you are making, and some Indian traditions read a woman’s left hand. [Which hand to read](/which-hand-to-read/) explains this.

## Left and right hand

Compare both hands. The life line often looks different on each: longer, fainter or broken on one side only. Tradition reads your writing hand as what you have built and the other hand as what you started with, so a difference is read as change, not as a problem. It is also common for photos of the two hands to be lit differently.
