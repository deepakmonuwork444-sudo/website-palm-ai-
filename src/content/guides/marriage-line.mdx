## Where is the marriage line?

The marriage line is one or more short lines on the outer edge of your palm, just under the little finger. They sit above the outer end of the heart line and run from the side of the hand a little way into the palm. Palmistry books place them on the Mount of Mercury, the pad under the little finger, which Indian readers call <Hi>बुध पर्वत</Hi> (Budh parvat). Dale’s *Indian Palmistry* (1895) describes them simply as “small lines on outside of hand on mount of Mercury”.

To find yours, close your hand loosely and look at its side, below the little finger. The small crosswise creases there are what palmistry calls marriage lines. In Hindi they are <Hi>विवाह रेखा</Hi> (vivah rekha) or <Hi>शादी की रेखा</Hi> (shadi ki rekha). English books also call them relationship lines, and Benham calls them lines of affection.

## What the marriage line can’t tell you

The marriage line can’t tell you when you’ll marry, whom you’ll marry, how many times, or whether a marriage will last. Nothing on a palm can. What the old books describe are bonds of feeling, and even there they disagree with each other, as the rest of this guide shows.

## What does the marriage line mean in palmistry?

In the old books, the marriage line is read as the mark of a deep bond with another person, not of a wedding day. Cheiro reads only the long, clearly formed lines as marriages or lasting unions. The short ones he reads as deep affection.

Cheiro is also clear that the hand does not record a ceremony. In *Cheiro’s Language of the Hand* (1900) he writes that “the hand does not recognize the mere fact of a ceremony, be it civil or religious”. He reads the lines as a record of the people who have strongly influenced your life. Benham goes further. He calls them lines of deep affection, not marriage lines. They appear, he notes, whether or not a legal marriage ever took place. He adds that they have nothing to do with love for your family.

### Marriage line age: why we don’t give one

Some old books do read an age from these lines. Cheiro reads an age at marriage from how close the line sits to the heart line. Benham also measures ages against the heart line. Dale’s book goes further and reads the number of lines as a number of partners.

We don’t repeat any of it. A crease a few millimetres long on the side of a hand can’t carry a date. So PalmSays never gives a marriage age, a count of marriages or a date.

## How many marriage lines should you have?

There is no right number. Having no marriage line, one, or two is common, and so is having three or more short ones. Benham notes that some hands show none of these lines and others show many.

The number is not a count of marriages. Dale’s book reads it that way. Benham, writing five years later, called predicting marriages from the number of lines unscientific, a habit that led to constant error. Having no marriage line does not mean you won’t marry, and two lines do not mean two marriages.

## Love marriage or arranged? No line can tell

No line on the palm can show whether a marriage will be a love marriage or an arranged one. It is one of the questions palm readers in India hear most, and the honest answer is short. How a marriage begins depends on people, families and choices, not on creases in the skin.

The old books themselves read these lines as bonds of feeling, not as the way two people meet. Markun’s *What You Should Know About Palmistry* (1927) even notes that fortune tellers look for other clues, such as a wedding ring. They “watch for such clues with very keen eyes”, he writes. If a reading claims to see love or arranged in your hand, it is going beyond anything a palm can show.

## The “divorce line” myth

There is no divorce line. The name comes from an old reading of a forked or broken marriage line, and we name it here only to take it apart.

Even the old books don’t agree on it. Cheiro reads a fork as a separation, while Benham said a fork does not always mean divorce. Markun opens his section by saying “the Marriage Lines appear to be somewhat uncertain”. He then lists the old readings, the forked line as divorce among them. He answers the last of them with a joke: “Perhaps it shows a sweet tooth, a tendency to write nonsense verse, or a limp in the left leg.”

We don’t read any marriage line as a divorce. A relationship depends on the two people in it: how they talk, choose and look after each other. If you are worried about a relationship, talk it through with the people in it, or with someone you trust.

## For women and men: the same line

The marriage line is read the same way for women and men. There is no separate marriage line for a woman, and no line describes a woman’s or a man’s fate. What traditions differ on is which hand to look at.

Tradition reads the hand you write with as the life you are making, and the other hand as what you started with. Dale’s *Indian Palmistry* records an Eastern custom of reading a man’s right palm and a woman’s left. Then it adds practical advice: read the hand where the lines show most clearly. [Which hand to read](/which-hand-to-read/) explains this in full, for female and male palm reading.

## Marriage line vs heart line

The marriage line and the heart line are different lines. The heart line is the long line across the top of your palm, just below the fingers. The marriage lines are the short marks on the edge of the hand, above its outer end.

If you want to know what palmistry says about how you love, look at your heart line, not the marriage line. Palmistry reads the heart line for your emotional style: how you feel, show affection and relate to people. The guide to the [heart line](/heart-line/) shows each of its types with its source.

## Can a photo show your marriage lines?

Usually not. Marriage lines sit on the side of the hand, running in from its outer edge. A normal palm photo, taken from above an open hand, sees them edge-on or not at all.

The small marks the books read on these lines, such as islands, crosses and stars, are far too small for a phone camera. Even a fork or a gap on a line this short is hard to tell apart from a crease made by bending the little finger.

That is why the PalmSays app does not read marriage lines. It reads the heart, head, life and fate lines, the sun line and the mounts, and it says so when a line isn’t clear. If you are curious, look at your marriage lines yourself in good light, as a reminder of the bonds that matter to you.
