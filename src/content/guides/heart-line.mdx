## Where is the heart line?

The heart line is the highest long line that runs across your palm. Look just below the base of your fingers. It starts on the little-finger edge of the hand and runs towards the index finger or the middle finger. Often it curves up at the end.

Don’t mix it up with the small creases at the base of each finger: those are finger creases, not palm lines. The next long line below the heart line starts from the thumb side. That one is the [head line](/head-line/). If you only see one long line across the top of your palm instead of two, read the section on [when the heart and head lines join](#when-the-heart-and-head-lines-join).

## Is the love line the same as the heart line?

Yes. “Love line” is simply another name for the heart line. In Hindi it is <Hi>हृदय रेखा</Hi> (hriday rekha). Older English books call it the mensal line, and Mrs Dale’s *Indian Palmistry* (1895) calls it the Line of Fortune. They all mean the same top line.

The small lines on the edge of your palm, under the little finger, are something else. Palmistry books call them marriage or relationship lines, and we explain them, honestly, in the [marriage line guide](/marriage-line/).

## Which end of the heart line do you read from?

Palmistry books disagree about this. Some read the heart line from the little-finger edge towards the index finger; others start from the index-finger end. So when two books read the same heart line in different ways, a careful reading shows you both instead of quietly picking one. That is what the PalmSays app does when its sources disagree.

In practice, most readings look at four things: **where the line ends**, **how long it is**, **how clearly it is marked**, and any **forks, breaks or branches**.

## Heart line types and their meanings

Each type below is a drawing of the shape the books describe, with the meaning written in our own words and the books it comes from. Your heart line may match more than one: a line can be long, forked and clearly marked all at once.

Here the traditions differ. Cheiro and Frith read feelings that are shown sparingly, not always put into words or gestures. That describes how feeling is shown, not how much is felt. Dale reads attachment that is slower to hold steady. Neither is a prediction about any relationship.

Most books don’t read the curve on its own. They read where the line ends. A straight line usually runs across towards the index-finger edge, so it is read like a long line; a line that curves up usually ends under a finger, so it is read by that ending (see above).

A fork at the finger end is one of the best-liked forms in the books: a balanced, honest and faithful style of affection, with ideals and everyday warmth held together. Jain adds a cheerful, generous nature, ready to help friends. A photo can’t always show exactly where a fork sits.

A broken heart line does not mean heartbreak is coming. Dale reads attachment that is slower to hold steady, and Cheiro’s general rule for broken lines is that where the two ends overlap, the line’s qualities carry on. Read it as a tendency, never an event.

A chained line looks like a row of small links. The books read attraction that forms easily and moves on easily, interest in people that shifts more than it settles. It is a pattern of attraction, not a judgement of character.

The form the classical books hold up as the well-made heart line, especially when it runs unbroken to the index finger: a warm, steady, affectionate nature with an even temper.

<Variation variant="heart-faint" title="Faint heart line" alt="A thin, faint heart line" src={['cheiro-palmistry-for-all-1916']}>

Feelings that run quietly under the surface, with a cooler, more detached manner in affection. It is a manner, not an absence of love. Light and focus also make lines look fainter in photos.

Small branches rising from the line towards the fingers. Markun reads them as friendship carrying real weight: warmth shared through close, supportive friendships.

## Marks on the heart line

The heart line can carry small marks: dots, islands, crosses, stars, squares or triangles. Books give each a meaning, but these marks are usually only a few pixels wide in a phone photo. That is too small to see reliably, so an honest reading leaves them out rather than guessing. What a good photo does show is a line’s **breaks**, **forks** and **branches**, and those are what the types above describe.

Some Indian readings talk about signs such as a trident (<Hi>त्रिशूल</Hi>) or a fish (<Hi>मछली</Hi>) on or near the heart line. We will cover those signs, and what the books actually say about them, in a separate guide.

## Heart line in the left and right hand, for women and men

The heart line is read the same way for everyone. There is no separate heart line for women or men. What changes is **which hand** you look at. Tradition reads the hand you write with as the life you are making, and the other hand as what you started with. Cheiro puts it as the right hand showing what a person has developed, and the left the natural tendencies. So it is worth comparing both: if the line looks different on each hand, the tradition reads that as a change from where you started.

[Which hand to read](/which-hand-to-read/) explains the traditions, including left-handed readers, in full.

## When the heart and head lines join

Sometimes the heart line and the head line don’t run as two lines. They form one straight crease across the palm. Palmistry calls it the simian line; doctors call it a single palmar crease. It is usually a normal variation. Cheiro reads a single line like this as great intensity of character, and says where it sits matters: at head-line height it leans to the mind, higher up to feeling. The [simian line guide](/simian-line/) covers the medical facts first, then what the books say.

## What the heart line can’t tell you

The heart line is read for *how* you tend to feel and show affection. It can’t say anything about who, when or whether.

## Myths about the heart line

<MythReality>
<Myth claim="A broken heart line means heartbreak or divorce.">

No line predicts a break-up. The books that read a broken heart line describe attachment that takes longer to settle: a tendency, not an event. And a crease or shadow can make a line look broken in a photo.

</Myth>
<Myth claim="A short heart line means you can’t love.">

The books read a short line as feelings shown sparingly. That is about how affection is shown, not how much is felt.

</Myth>
<Myth claim="The heart line shows how healthy your heart is.">

Some old books say this. The app’s rule set leaves out every health claim they make, and so do we: palm lines are not a health test.

</Myth>
</MythReality>
