## Finger by finger: the planet names

Palmistry names each finger after a planet, and the pad just below it, its mount, carries the same name. In Hindi the fingers are the <Hi>अंगूठा</Hi> (thumb), <Hi>तर्जनी</Hi> (index), <Hi>मध्यमा</Hi> (middle), <Hi>अनामिका</Hi> (ring) and <Hi>कनिष्ठा</Hi> (little).

### Index finger: the finger of Jupiter

Cheiro’s *Palmistry for All* (1916) reads a long index finger as a love of leading and of taking charge of others, and a short one as a dislike of responsibility. An index finger almost as long as the middle finger is read as pride.

### Middle finger: the finger of Saturn

A long middle finger is read as prudence and a reserved, studious nature, with a liking for time alone. A short one is read as a lighter, less serious manner.

### Ring finger: the finger of the Sun

A long ring finger is read as a love of beauty and a wish to be recognised. When it is very long, Cheiro reads a liking for risk and speculation. A short one is read as caring little for these things.

### Little finger: the finger of Mercury

A long little finger, reaching past the top joint of the ring finger, is read as a gift for expression, in speaking and in writing. Cheiro reads a short one as finding it harder to put thoughts into words, and adds that such people often write better than they speak.

Cheiro also notes that a finger which leans towards its neighbour takes on some of that finger’s qualities. The pads under the fingers are read with them in [palm mounts](/palm-mounts/). New to these words? The [palmistry terms](/palmistry-terms/#phalanges) glossary explains each one in a sentence.

## Is your index finger longer than your ring finger?

This is the comparison people ask about most. Hold your fingers together and compare both tips against the middle finger.

<HandDrawings set="fingers" />

#### Index finger longer than the ring finger

The index finger is the finger of Jupiter. When it is the longer of the two, palmistry reads it as a drive to lead and to take charge.

#### Ring finger longer than the index finger

The ring finger is the finger of the Sun, or Apollo. When it is the longer of the two, palmistry reads it as a love of beauty and art, and a wish to be recognised for what you do. This is the most common result: in most hands the ring finger is a little longer.

#### Index and ring fingers the same length

Cheiro reads index and ring fingers of equal length as a sign of a balanced mind, and calls it rare. The books disagree here: Heron-Allen (1885) and Cheiro’s own earlier book read equal fingers as a wish to shine in art.

Scientists study the same comparison as the “2D:4D” digit ratio. A review that pooled 116 samples of expert-measured fingers (Hönekopp and Watson, 2010) found that, on average, men’s ring fingers are a little longer compared with their index fingers than women’s. That is an average difference between large groups, not a reading of one person, and it does not make palmistry a science. [Is palmistry real?](/is-palmistry-real/) looks at the evidence.

## Are your fingers long or short for your palm?

Cheiro reads long fingers as a love of detail: noticing small things, and sometimes worrying over them. Short fingers are read as quick and impulsive, taking things in as a whole and not fussing over detail.

Markun (1927) describes how the old books measured this: the middle finger from its tip to the knuckle on the back of the hand, the palm from the base of that finger to the wrist. A finger about as long as the palm was taken as a sign of balance. Palm shape and finger length together give your [hand types in palmistry](/hand-types/).

## The thumb in palmistry

Cheiro calls the thumb as important to the hand as the nose is to the face, and reads it for natural will power. Its three parts stand for three things: the top section (with the nail) for **will**, the middle section for **logic**, and the base, covered by the mount of Venus, for **warmth**. A larger part is read as a stronger quality.

### How far does your thumb open?

- **Held close to the hand:** a careful, reserved nature that keeps its thoughts to itself.
- **A moderate opening:** independent without going to extremes. Cheiro says a thumb should neither stand at a right angle nor lie too close.
- **Wide open:** independence of will and fearlessness. Near a right angle, the books read a nature that goes to extremes.

### How long is your thumb?

A long thumb is read as a strong will, guided by reason. A shorter thumb is read as being guided more by feeling than by reasoning. Thumbs often turn sideways to the camera, which makes them look shorter in a photo.

### Is the top joint of your thumb supple or firm?

Cheiro sorts thumbs into two classes. A **supple-jointed** thumb bends back at the top joint: an adaptable, broad-minded nature, generous and quick to give in. A **firm-jointed** thumb stays straight: a more resisting nature that thinks before agreeing and holds to its view once decided. He also reads a middle section that narrows like a waist as tact, and a straight, full one as a preference for argument and reason.

## The three sections of each finger (phalanges)

Each finger has three sections, called phalanges. *Cheiro’s Guide to the Hand* (1900) reads them from the tip down as three worlds: the top section for **ideals**, the middle for **reason**, and the base for **material things**. A long top section is read as a love of ideals; a large middle one as a love of reason; a long base section as a love of material things. A full, thick base is read as enjoying good food and comfort; a slim, waisted one as caring little for luxury.

## Knotty fingers or smooth fingers: the joints

*Cheiro’s Language of the Hand* reads **smooth** joints as impulsive and intuitive, quick to reach a conclusion without working through each step. **Knotty** joints, where the knuckles stand out, are read as method and analysis: they “stop the tide of impulse”, in his words, and make a mind more observant and thoughtful. He adds that hard work neither makes nor removes them.

## Gaps between the fingers

Spread your hand the way it opens naturally. Cheiro reads a wide gap between:

- **the thumb and index finger:** independence of will and fearlessness;
- **the index and middle fingers:** independence of thought;
- **the middle and ring fingers:** independence from circumstances;
- **the ring and little fingers:** independence in action.

Fingers held close together are read as a respect for custom and for what other people think. Fingers that bend back easily are read as quick to grasp ideas; fingers that curve inwards as slower to take up an idea but holding on to it. Older books disagree about the gaps: Heron-Allen (1885) and Frith (1895) read them as curiosity instead.

## Lines on the fingers

The short creases across each finger joint are flexion creases, the places where the skin folds when the finger bends, the same kind of crease as the major palm lines. They form before birth (Kimura and Kitagawa, 1986). Older books also read fine lines on the finger sections: upright lines as helpful, crossing lines as obstacles, and particular patterns as particular events. Those are forecasts, and even Markun’s 1927 book tells them as tales, so we leave them out. Fine lines on the fingers rarely show clearly in a phone photo either.

For the lines on the palm itself, start with [how to read palm lines](/palm-reading/).

## Myths about the fingers in palmistry

<MythReality>
<Myth claim="A long ring finger means you will be rich, or a gambler.">

The books read a love of beauty, a wish to be recognised and, when the finger is very long, a liking for risk. That is a tendency, not a forecast about money.

</Myth>
<Myth claim="Finger length proves palmistry is scientific.">

Digit-ratio research compares averages across large groups of people. It does not support reading one person’s character or future from their fingers.

</Myth>
<Myth claim="The lines on your fingers foretell events.">

They are creases where the finger bends. The old readings of them are forecasts that no line can make.

</Myth>
</MythReality>
